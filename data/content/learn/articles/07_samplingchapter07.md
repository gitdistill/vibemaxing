# Sampling Tutorial 7: Sampling Review
## A sampling exercise
In this chapter we suggest an exercise to help you check your understanding of how to sample and play audio. Try completing this exercise in a new file of your own before you check the solution given in the example patch. (But don't have the example Patcher open while you design your own patch, or you will hear both patches when you turn audio on.) The exercise is to design a patch in which:
  * Typing the _a_ key on the computer keyboard turns audio on. Typing _a_ again toggles audio off.
  * Typing _r_ on the computer keyboard makes a one-second recording of whatever audio is coming into the computer.
  * Typing _p_ plays the recording. Playback is to be at half speed, so that the sound lasts two seconds.
  * An amplitude envelope is applied to the sample when it is played, tapering the amplitude slightly at the beginning and end so that there are no sudden clicks heard at either end of the sample.
  * The sample is played back with a 3 Hz vibrato added to it. The depth of the vibrato is one semitone up and down (a factor of 2
±1/12
).


## Hints
You will need to store the sound in a [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") and play it back from memory.
You can record directly into the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") with [record~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/record~/ "record~"). (See _[Record and Play Samples](https://docs.cycling74.com/learn/articles/07_samplingchapter01/)_.) The input to [record~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/record~/ "record~") will come from [adc~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/adc~/ "adc~") (or [ezadc~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/ezadc~/ "ezadc~")).
The two obvious choices for playing a sample from a [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") at half speed are [play~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/play~/ "play~") and [groove~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/groove~/ "groove~"). However, because we want to add vibrato to the sound - by continuously varying the playback speed - the better choice is [groove~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/groove~/ "groove~"), which uses a (possibly time-varying) signal to control its playback speed directly. (See _["Playback with Loops"](https://docs.cycling74.com/learn/articles/07_samplingchapter03/)_.)
The amplitude envelope is best generated by a [line~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/line~/ "line~") object which is sending its output to a [*~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/*~/ "*~") object to scale the amplitude of the output signal (coming from [groove~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/groove~/ "groove~")). You might want to use a [function](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/function/ "function") object to draw the envelope, and send its output to [line~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/line~/ "line~") to describe the envelope. (See _["Additive Synthesis"](https://docs.cycling74.com/learn/articles/06_synthesischapter01/)_.)
The computer keyboard will need to trigger messages to the [adc~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/adc~/ "adc~"), [record~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/record~/ "record~"), [groove~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/groove~/ "groove~"), and [line~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/line~/ "line~") (or [function](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/function/ "function")) objects in order to perform the required tasks. Use the [key](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/key/ "key") object to get the keystrokes, and use [select](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/select/ "select") to detect the keys you want to use.
Use a sinusoidal wave from a [cycle~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/cycle~/ "cycle~") object to apply vibrato to the sample. The frequency of the [cycle~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/cycle~/ "cycle~") will determine the _rate_ of the vibrato, and the amplitude of the sinusoid will determine the _depth_ of vibrato. Therefore, you will need to scale the [cycle~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/cycle~/ "cycle~") object's amplitude with a [*~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/*~/ "*~") object to achieve the proper vibrato depth.
In the discussion of vibrato in the synthesis tutorials, we created vibrato by adding the output of the modulating oscillator to the frequency input of the carrier oscillator. However, two things are different in this exercise. First of all, the modulating oscillator needs to modulate the playback speed of [groove~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/groove~/ "groove~") rather than the frequency of another [cycle~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/cycle~/ "cycle~") object. Second, adding the output of the modulator to the input of the carrier - as in _["Vibrato and Frequency Modulation"](https://docs.cycling74.com/learn/articles/06_synthesischapter04/)_ - creates a vibrato of equal _frequency_ above and below the carrier frequency, but does not create a vibrato of equal _pitch_ up and down (as required in this exercise). A change in pitch is achieved by _multiplying_ the carrier frequency by a certain amount, rather than by adding an amount to it.
To raise the pitch of a tone by one semitone, you must multiply its frequency by the twelfth root of 2, which is a factor of 2 to the1/12power (approximately 1.06). To lower the pitch of a tone by one semitone, you must multiply its frequency by 2 to the-1/12power (approximately 0.944). To calculate a signal value that changes continuously within this range, you may need to use an MSP object not yet discussed, [pow~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/pow~/ "pow~"). Consult its description in the Objects section of this manual for details.
## Solution
  * Scroll the example Patcher window all the way to the right to see a solution to this exercise.


The arguments to the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") object specify a length in milliseconds (`1000`) and a number of channels (`2`). This determines how much memory will initially be allocated to the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~").
Since the memory allocated in the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") is limited to one second, there is no need to tell the [record~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/record~/ "record~") object to stop when you record into the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~"). It stops when it reaches the end of the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~").
The keystrokes from the computer keyboard are reported by [key](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/key/ "key"), and the [select](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/select/ "select") object is used to detect the _a_ , _r_ , and _p_ keys. The `bang` s from [select](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/select/ "select") trigger the necessary messages to [adc~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/adc~/ "adc~"), [record~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/record~/ "record~"), and [groove~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/groove~/ "groove~").
The keystroke _p_ is also used to trigger the amplitude envelope at the same time as the sample is played. This envelope is used to scale the output of [groove~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/groove~/ "groove~").
A [sig~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/sig~/ "sig~")`0.5` object sets the basic playback speed of [groove~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/groove~/ "groove~") at half speed. The amplitude of a 3 Hz cosine wave is scaled by a factor of 0.083333 (equal to1/12, but more computationally efficient than dividing by 12) so that it varies from-1/12to 1/12. This sinusoidal signal is used as the exponent in a power function in [pow~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/pow~/ "pow~") (2 to the power of the input), and the result is used as the factor by which to multiply the playback speed.
