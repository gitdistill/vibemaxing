# Sampling Tutorial 1: Recording and Playback
The next group of tutorials introduce concepts for the playback within MSP of audio _samples_. Samples can be recorded and played back in a variety of ways, depending on your requirements, and can be accessed either from computer memory or as an audio file on your hard drive.
## Sound input: adc~
Just as the [dac~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/dac~/ "dac~") object exists for getting sound out of your computer (to your headphones or speakers), an object exists for getting audio from the real world _into_ your computer: the analog-to-digital conversion object called [adc~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/adc~/ "adc~"). It recognizes all the same messages as the [dac~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/dac~/ "dac~") object, but instead of sending signal to the audio output jacks of the computer, [adc~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/adc~/ "adc~") receives signal from the audio input jacks, and sends the incoming signal out its outlets. Just as [dac~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/dac~/ "dac~") has a user interface version called [ezdac~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/ezdac~/ "ezdac~"), there is an iconic version of [adc~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/adc~/ "adc~") called [ezadc~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/ezadc~/ "ezadc~").
![](https://docs.cycling74.com/images/a8aa8d60ab1745cc66cbe82273e700d1_280.webp)
_adc~ and ezadc~ get sound from the audio input jacks and send it out as a signal_
To use the [adc~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/adc~/ "adc~") object, you need to send sound from some source into the computer. The sound may come from any line level source such as a media player, or from a microphone - your computer might have a built-in microphone, or you can use a standard microphone via a preamplifer. If you have a external audio interface, you can select it from the Audio Status window and use the inputs on that device as well.
  * Double click on the [adc~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/adc~/ "adc~") object to open the Audio Status window. Make sure that the _Input Source_ popup menu displays the input device you want. Depending on your computer system, audio card and driver, you may not have a choice of input device-this is nothing to be concerned about.
  * In the section of the tutorial patcher labeled `1`, click on the toggle above the [adc~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/adc~/ "adc~") object to turn audio on. If you want to hear the input sound played directly out the output jacks, adjust the [gain~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/gain~/ "gain~") object marked _Audio thruput level_. If you are using the internal microphone on your computer, be careful for feedback when playing sound out of the built-in speakers at the same time.


## Storing audio in MSP: buffer~
In the lower part of the patch notice the object called [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~"). Though it isn't connected to the rest of the patcher logic, the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") object provides the central functionality of this patcher - it stores the sample data from the sound we'll be working with. In MSP, all sample playback from computer memory takes place via [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") objects. The object is created with a `name` as its argument, which is arbitrary. This name is used by other objects to _access_ the audio data in the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") object. So in our case, our [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") is named `soundbyte`; any objects in the patcher which need to play or record into that chunk of sample memory must also be set to use `soundbyte` as their [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") name. The second argument to [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") sets the initial length of the sample in milliseconds. In our tutorial, we'll be working with a 2-second (2000 millisecond) sample of audio.
## Recording a sound: record~
A variety of objects exist to access the data in a [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~"). In the section of the patch labeled `2` (in the upper-left), the [record~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/record~/ "record~") object takes an MSP signal and _writes_ it into the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") designated by the object's argument.
When [record~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/record~/ "record~") receives a non-zero integer in its left inlet, it begins recording the signal connected to its record inlet; a `0` stops the recording. You can specify recording start and end points within the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") by sending numbers in the two right inlets of [record~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/record~/ "record~"). If you don't specify start and end points, recording will fill the entire [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~"). Notice that the length of the recording is limited by the length of the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~").
In the tutorial patch, [record~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/record~/ "record~") will stop recording after 2 seconds (2000 ms). We have included a delayed `bang` to turn off the [toggle](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/toggle/ "toggle") after two seconds, but this is just to make the [toggle](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/toggle/ "toggle") accurately display the state of [record~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/record~/ "record~"). It is not necessary to stop [record~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/record~/ "record~") explicitly, because it will stop automatically when it reaches its end point or the end of the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~").
  * Make sure that you have sound coming into the computer, then click on the [toggle](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/toggle/ "toggle") to record two seconds of the incoming sound. If you want to, you can double-click on the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") afterward to see the recorded signal.


## Reading through a buffer~: index~
Once you get sound into a [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") object, there are several objects that exist in MSP to _read_ the data back out as an MSP signal. In addition, you can save your record to an you an audio file by clicking the `write` message attached to the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") in the lower-right.
The simplest way to play back the contents of a [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") is to use the [index~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/index~/ "index~") object, shown in the area of the tutorial patcher labeled `3`. The [index~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/index~/ "index~") object receives a `signal` as its input, which represents a sample number. It looks up that sample in its associated [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~"), and sends the value of that sample out its outlet as a signal. The [count~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/count~/ "count~") object just sends out a signal value that increases by one with each sample. So, if you send the output of [count~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/count~/ "count~") - a steady stream of increasing numbers - to the input of [index~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/index~/ "index~") - which will treat them as sample numbers - [index~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/index~/ "index~") will read straight through the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~"), playing it back at the current sampling rate.
  * Turn up the [gain~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/gain~/ "gain~") slider labeled `Indexed level`, and click on the [button](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/button/ "button") marked `Play` to play the sound in the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~"). You can change the starting sample number by sending a different starting number into [count~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/count~/ "count~"). Remember that these values are in _samples_ , not milliseconds, so depending on your audio hardware's sampling rate different numbers will mean different things. For example, to play the sample starting halfway into the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") on regular CD-quality computer hardware, you would use the value `44100`.


## Variable speed playback: play~
While the [index~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/index~/ "index~") object is a useful one to simply play a sample forwards at normal speed, part of the creative fun of sampling is the ability to manipulate sample plackback in different ways. The [play~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/play~/ "play~") object (shown in the section of the patcher labeled `4` allows you to access a [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") object's contents much as if it were an analogue tape, with the signal input to [play~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/play~/ "play~") setting the position of the play head.
As with [index~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/index~/ "index~"), the [play~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/play~/ "play~") object receives a signal in its inlet which indicates a position which it uses to play audio out of its associated [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~"). The [play~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/play~/ "play~") object differs from [index~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/index~/ "index~") in two important ways, however. First, the unit of time for the [play~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/play~/ "play~") object is in milliseconds, allowing for an easier understanding of where you're playing in the sample. Second, when [play~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/play~/ "play~") receives a position that falls between two samples in the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") it interpolates between those two values. For this reason, you can read through a [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") at any speed by sending an increasing or decreasing signal to [play~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/play~/ "play~"), and it will interpolate between samples as necessary.
Because the [play~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/play~/ "play~") object puts out the sample value that correlates to the time given at its signal inlet, in order to hear anything, that time needs to be changing constantly; this is similar to the way a tape recorder works... a constant signal sent to a [play~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/play~/ "play~") object is the equivalent of a tape record set to "pause". The most obvious way to use the [play~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/play~/ "play~") object is to send it a linearly increasing (or decreasing) signal from a [line~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/line~/ "line~") object, as shown in the tutorial patcher.
Reading from 0 to 2000 (millisecond position in the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~")) in a time of 2000 ms produces normal playback. Reading from 0 to 2000 in 4000 ms produces half-speed playback, and so on. Generating a ramp with the [line~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/line~/ "line~") object that runs from high to low (e.g. from 2000 ms back to 0 ms) will play the audio backwards.
  * Click on the different [message](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/message/ "message") box objects to hear the sound played in various speed/direction combinations. Turn audio off when you have finished.


Although not demonstrated in this tutorial patch, it's worth noting that you could use other signals as input to [play~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/play~/ "play~") in order to achieve accelerations and decelerations, such as an exponential curve from a [curve~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/curve~/ "curve~") object or even an appropriately scaled sinusoid from a [cycle~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/cycle~/ "cycle~") object.
## Summary
Sound coming into the computer enters MSP via the [adc~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/adc~/ "adc~") object. The [record~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/record~/ "record~") object stores the incoming sound - or any other signal -- in a [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~"). You can record into the entire [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~"), or you can record into any portion of it by specifying start and end buffer positions in the two rightmost inlets of [record~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/record~/ "record~"). For simple normal-speed playback of the sound in a [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~"), you can use the [count~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/count~/ "count~") and [index~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/index~/ "index~") objects to read through it at the current sampling rate. Use the [line~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/line~/ "line~") and [play~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/play~/ "play~") objects for variable-speed playback and/or for reading through the [buffer~](https://docs.cycling74.com/reference/buffer~/ "buffer~") in both directions.
